Tough questions

Time15 min
DifficultyEasy
Steps6 questions
You will needNothing, read and discuss

The questions people use to poke holes in the method. Only what is true.

You typed the seed on a laptop. Isn’t that exposed?

Typed on Tails, networking off, one seed per boot, then a full shutdown. Tails forgets the session. A hardware keylogger is still possible. But one captured seed still can’t spend: the fund needs two. Never type two seeds on one boot.

Why not start with a hardware wallet?

A signer can join later; it is not how these keys are made. The randomness is dice you can watch. No chip to trust, no firmware to audit.

Is dice randomness weaker than a hardware RNG?

No. 256 bits is 256 bits, whatever made them.

If the map passphrase is lost, are the coins gone?

Not with all three plates. They rebuild the map exactly. But two plates plus a lost passphrase means the addresses are unrecoverable. Back up the passphrase paper in two places, away from the plates and USBs.

What if someone finds one plate?

They can’t spend. One key alone opens nothing. But never keep two plates in one place: that’s the whole lock in one drawer.

Shouldn’t I add a passphrase to the multisig?

No. The security is the distribution, not one more secret. A passphrase on the 2-of-3 is a single point of failure in disguise. Lose it and two plates aren’t enough. The home plate’s small decoy wallet is the separate, optional place for a passphrase.

That is the whole method. Everything else is commentary.
