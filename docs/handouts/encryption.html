You will need: descriptor-tool.html (verified) · A dice passphrase

At a glance. Time: 30 minutes. Difficulty: Careful. Steps: 5.

  1. Boot Tails. Copy the tool and the map.
  2. Open the tool. Encrypt the map.
  3. Copy the locked file. Delete the plain one.
  4. Write the passphrase on paper. Twice.
  5. Prove it opens. Check the first address.

The map can watch the money, but it cannot spend. Anyone who can read it can see the balance, so it never sits around as plain text.

Files used: Tails 7.16 or later 7.x (verified).

Get the descriptor tool

Download the descriptor tool page. Save it. Check its fingerprint on the verify page before you copy it to your data USB.

Open descriptor tool (HTML)

The Python script is the backup check. It lives in the kit.

Make a passphrase with dice

Roll five dice. Read the faces left to right. Look the five-digit number up in the EFF word list. Write the word down. Do this six times. Six words give about 77 bits.

Use the word list or the Diceware mapper from a saved copy, on Tails with networking disabled. Never in an everyday browser.

Watch how it works:

Open the demo full screen

Write the six words on paper, in two separate places. Never stamp a passphrase on a plate. Never keep it with the plate, the map, or the USBs.

Seed words and passphrase words use different dice methods. Seeds keep faces 1 to 4 and reroll 5 and 6. Passphrases keep all six faces, no rerolls. A passphrase word is never a seed word.

Step 1 · Boot Tails. Copy the tool and the map.

Open the demo full screen

Boot Tails, networking Disabled. Copy the tool page and map.txt to the Desktop.

Step 2 · Open the tool. Encrypt the map.

Open the demo full screen

Open the tool page. It checks the computer is offline before it starts. Choose Encrypt a map. Drop map.txt on the page or paste the text. Enter your passphrase twice. Review, then Encrypt. Download map.txt.asc.

Step 3 · Copy the locked file. Delete the plain one.

Copy map.txt.asc to two USB drives. Delete map.txt from the Desktop and the USB. Empty the trash.

Step 4 · Write the passphrase on paper. Twice.

Two copies, two separate places. This paper unlocks every encrypted copy of the map. Treat it like a plate.

Step 5 · Prove it opens. Check the first address.

On a later offline boot, open the tool again. Choose Decrypt a map. Paste the armored text. Enter the passphrase once. The restored map must match, and the first address must match what you wrote down.

If the passphrase is lost, the coins aren't gone, as long as all three plates still exist. Three seeds rebuild the map. Two cannot.

Stop if

  • The tool says the computer is online: stop. Do not type the passphrase.
  • You want the xpubs on paper "just in case": that's a leak. Stop.
  • You want to invent your own cipher: stop. Use the tool.
