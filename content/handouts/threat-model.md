# Before you begin

Don't trust, verify. That starts with knowing **what you're defending against**. Five minutes now decides whether 2-of-3 across three places is overkill for you, or exactly enough. Check every threat you actually care about. The guide answers each one under its checkbox.

**The seed phrase is the wallet.** The hardware device is a signing tool, nothing more. Anyone who holds the words holds the coins, and no device in the world can stop them. Everything in this guide follows from that.

## What am I protecting against?

- ☐ **Theft:** burglar, coercion, the $5 wrench
  - *How the guide answers it:* 2-of-3 means one stolen seed buys an attacker nothing. They need a second, in another place.
- ☐ **Fire, flood, disaster:** one location stops existing
  - *How the guide answers it:* three places, stamped titanium. The two surviving plates plus the wallet map rebuild the wallet.
- ☐ **My own mistakes:** lost paper, copied a word wrong
  - *How the guide answers it:* two-tool agreement on every word before anything is stamped; the plates hold what survived verification.
- ☐ **Scams:** an email or a site that looks official asks for my words
  - *How the guide answers it:* the words go into the device itself, during recovery, and nowhere else. Never a website, never an app, never a form from an email. No hardware company will ever ask for your seed phrase. If a message asks, the message is the attack, no matter how official it looks.
- ☐ **Death or incapacitation:** heirs who aren't Bitcoiners
  - *How the guide answers it:* the inheritance sheet. Written for a non-Bitcoiner, kept where your family can find it.

> [!NOTE] **Read it back:** if you checked theft and disaster, the 2-of-3 method exists for you. If you checked scams, or your coins sit somewhere with a seed you do not fully control, the single-sig sheet exists for you. Either way, the rest of the guide is the long version of these answers.

## Where are your coins right now?

Check the one that is true today. Each answer has a door.

- ☐ **On an exchange.** The exchange holds the keys, so the exchange holds the coins. Your job is to bring them home. Either build the full 2-of-3 from the checklist, or take the single-sig sheet if one key done right is what you will actually finish.
- ☐ **In a phone app or software wallet.** Your words have lived on a connected device. Treat that seed as exposed, even if nothing has gone wrong yet. The [single-sig sheet](/handouts/single-sig) walks you through making a fresh seed the offline way and moving the coins onto it. Your old device can stay on as the signer.
- ☐ **On a hardware wallet, one seed.** You are closer than you think. The [single-sig sheet](/handouts/single-sig) is your upgrade path: check how the seed was born, add a passphrase, stamp the words in metal, and set up a watch-only wallet on your computer.
- ☐ **Nowhere yet.** Start at the beginning. The checklist, then the keys. The full 2-of-3 is the destination this guide recommends.
