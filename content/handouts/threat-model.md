# Before you begin

Don't trust, verify.

- **The seed phrase is the wallet.** The device only signs.
- Anyone who holds the words holds the coins. No device can stop them.
- Check every threat you actually care about. The guide answers each one under its checkbox.

::fig threat-model/two-to-open

## What am I protecting against?

- ☐ **Theft:** burglar, coercion, the $5 wrench
  - One stolen seed buys an attacker nothing. They need a second, in another place.
- ☐ **Fire, flood, disaster:** one location stops existing
  - Three places, stamped titanium. Two surviving plates plus the map rebuild the wallet.
- ☐ **My own mistakes:** lost paper, copied a word wrong
  - Two tools must agree on every word before anything is stamped.
- ☐ **Scams:** an email or a site that looks official asks for my words
  - Words into the device itself, during recovery, nowhere else. No hardware company will ever ask for your seed phrase. A message that asks is the attack.
- ☐ **Death or incapacitation:** heirs who aren't Bitcoiners
  - The inheritance sheet, written for a non-Bitcoiner, kept where your family can find it.

> [!NOTE] **Read it back:** checked theft and disaster, the 2-of-3 is built for you. Checked scams, or coins somewhere with a seed you do not fully control, the single-sig sheet is your door.

## Where are your coins right now?

- ☐ **On an exchange.** The exchange holds the keys, so the exchange holds the coins.
- ☐ **In a phone app or software wallet.** Your words have lived on a connected device. Treat that seed as exposed. This makes a fresh seed the offline way and moves the coins onto it. Your old device can stay on as the signer.
- ☐ **On a hardware wallet, one seed.** You are closer than you think. This is your upgrade path: check how the seed was born, add a passphrase, stamp the words in metal, set up a watch-only wallet.
- ☐ **Nowhere yet.** Start at the beginning. Work the steps.
